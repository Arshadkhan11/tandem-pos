For writers

The Tandem Writer's Guide

Everything you need to write, polish and publish a post, even if it is your first one.

The 5-minute version
  1. Sign in at tandemretreat.com/write (bookmark it) and click Add post.
  2. Type the Title, then write the Story.
  3. Add a cover image and describe it.
  4. Fill in Google and sharing (title, description, main keyword).
  5. Save as Draft, click Open the post to preview, then set Published and save.

1. Sign in and find your way around

  • Go to tandemretreat.com/write and sign in with the username and password you were given. The first time, click Change password (top of the page) and choose your own.
  • You will see one section: Blog → Posts. That is everything you need. The list shows each post's State: Live (visitors can read it), Scheduled (will go live later) or Draft (only signed-in staff can see it).
  • Click Add post to start, or click a title to edit an existing post.
  • You will see three save buttons. Save and continue editing is the one to use while writing: it saves and keeps you on the page, like pressing Ctrl+S. Save saves and returns to the list.
  • You can add and edit posts but not delete them. To take a post down, change its status back to Draft.

2. Choose a topic and a keyword

Do this before you open the editor. A good post answers one question that real people type into Google.

  1. Think like a visitor. Someone in Bengaluru is planning a trip to Nandi Hills: How do I get there? When should I leave? Where is the best sunrise view? Where can we eat?
  2. Pick one main keyword per post. This is the phrase you want the post to be found for, for example nandi hills sunrise points. One post, one main keyword.
  3. Check it on Google. Type it in. Look at the suggestions that appear as you type and the People also ask box. Open the top results and ask: what do they miss? You win by being more helpful, more local and more up to date.
  4. Always include "Nandi Hills". The word "Tandem" on its own is crowded (there is a famous language app with that name), so aim for phrases visitors actually search, such as things to do near Nandi Hills.

Ten good first posts (check every fact, such as timings, fees and distances, before you publish):

  1. How to reach Nandi Hills from Bengaluru: routes, timings and parking
  2. Nandi Hills sunrise: the best viewpoints and when to arrive
  3. The best time to visit Nandi Hills, month by month
  4. Things to do near Nandi Hills: a one-day plan
  5. A weekend itinerary from Bengaluru
  6. Nandi Hills at sunset: where to go
  7. Where to eat on Nandi Hills
  8. Nearby places worth adding to your trip
  9. Nandi Hills for couples, families and photographers
  10. Entry rules, timings and what to carry (keep this one updated)

3. Write the post

The three boxes at the top

BoxWhat to put there
TitleThe headline readers see. Clear beats clever. Include the main keyword near the start if it reads naturally.
StoryThe post itself, written in the editor below.
Short summaryOne or two sentences shown under the title and on the Journal page. It is the "why read this" line.

The editor toolbar

  • Paragraph ▾: choose Heading for a section title or Subheading for a smaller one inside a section. Use headings to break up the text.
  • B and I: bold and italic. Use sparingly, for key facts only.
  • Link: select some words, click the link button, paste the web address and press Enter.
  • Quote: for a standout line or a guest's words.
  • Bulleted and numbered lists: perfect for timings, what to carry, and step-by-step directions.
  • Image and media buttons: see Part 4. The media button embeds a YouTube video when you paste its link.
  • Undo / Redo: made a mistake? Press Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on a Mac). The word count is shown under the editor.

Writing in Google Docs or Word first? Paste with Ctrl+Shift+V (Cmd+Shift+V on a Mac), which pastes plain text. Then add headings and bold here so the formatting stays clean.

A simple structure that works

  1. Opening (2–3 sentences). Say what the reader will get, and use your main keyword in the first paragraph.
  2. 3–6 sections, each with a Heading that answers a question (for example "What time should we leave Bengaluru?").
  3. A practical-info list: timings, distance, parking, what to carry.
  4. A short Q&A with the two or three things people always ask.
  5. A warm closing with a gentle invitation, such as "After the viewpoint, come hungry: a table at Tandem is a short walk away." Be helpful first and promotional second.

Writing tips

  • Short paragraphs (2–4 lines). Most readers are on phones.
  • Be specific and first-hand. "We reached at 5:40 am and the east viewpoint was empty" beats "go early for good views".
  • Use plain words, one idea per paragraph, and read it aloud before publishing.
  • Link to other Tandem pages using natural words, such as "our guide to the best sunrise points", never "click here".
  • Only state prices, timings and rules you have confirmed. If unsure, leave it out.

4. Add images

The cover image (shown at the top and in lists)

  1. Scroll to the Cover image section and click Choose file.
  2. In Cover image description, describe the picture in a sentence, for example "Golden sunrise over the hills from the Nandi Hills viewpoint".

Pictures inside the story

  1. Click in the text where the picture should go.
  2. Click the image button in the toolbar and upload from your computer. You can also drag a file into the editor or paste a copied image.
  3. Click the picture. A small toolbar appears. Use Change image text alternative to describe the picture (this is the "alt text"), and the alignment buttons to place it.

Rules for images

  • Only use pictures that are yours, that Tandem owns, or that carry a free licence with credit given. Never copy pictures from Google, Instagram or other blogs. Ask permission before featuring people you can recognise.
  • Types and size: JPG, PNG or WebP. The limit is 8 MB, but aim for under 500 KB. Heavy images make pages slow, and slow pages lose readers and rank lower.
  • Dimensions: cover image about 1600 × 900 px (landscape); pictures inside the story about 1200 px wide are plenty. Free tools to shrink pictures: Squoosh (squoosh.app) or TinyPNG (tinypng.com).
  • Name the file first. Rename IMG_4821.JPG to nandi-hills-sunrise-viewpoint.jpg before uploading (lowercase, words joined with hyphens).
  • Describe every picture. One natural sentence about what is in it. Do not start with "image of", and do not cram in keywords.
  • iPhone photos: iPhones often save pictures as HEIC, which will not upload. Send or export the photo as a JPG first.

5. The "Google and sharing" section

This is where you control how the post looks in Google. As you type, a live preview shows the result, and a checklist shows what to improve. It is a guide, not a score to chase: ignore a tick that does not make sense for your post.

FieldHow to fill it in
Google titleThe blue headline. Aim for 50–60 characters with the main keyword near the start. Example: Nandi Hills Sunrise Points: Best Viewpoints & Timings | Tandem. Leave it empty to use the post title.
Google descriptionThe grey text under it. 110–155 characters: say what the post gives the reader and why to click. Use the keyword once.
Web address endingThe last part of the link. Short, lowercase, hyphens, with the keyword: nandi-hills-sunrise-points. It fills in from the title; trim it. Do not change it after publishing, because old links would break.
Main keywordThe phrase you chose in Part 2. Only the checklist uses it; visitors never see it.
Hide from GoogleTick only for posts that should not appear in search, for example a test post.

The checklist looks at: title and description length, enough words, at least one heading, picture descriptions, links to other Tandem pages, and whether your keyword appears in the title, address, description and first paragraph.

6. Save, preview and publish

  1. Leave Status on Draft while you work and click Save and continue editing often.
  2. Under Publishing, click Open the post. It opens exactly as readers will see it, with a "Preview" note that only signed-in staff see. Check it on your phone too.
  3. Proofread, then run the checklist in Part 8.
  4. Change Status to Published and save. The post is live immediately.
  5. To publish later, set a future Publish date and time with status Published. The post appears automatically at that time and shows as Scheduled until then.

After you publish

  • Open the live link in a private window to confirm it looks right.
  • Share it as an Update on the Tandem Google Business Profile, plus Instagram and WhatsApp.
  • Add a link to the new post from one or two older posts.
  • Ask the site owner to submit the link in Google Search Console ("Request indexing") so Google finds it sooner.
  • Updating an old post? Edit it and save; keep the same web address. For time-sensitive posts, mention the month in the opening ("Updated October 2026").

7. What helps a post rank on Google

Nobody can promise a number-one spot, and a new site usually needs weeks or months to be noticed. These are the things that genuinely help:

  1. Answer the question completely. Give the answer early, then add depth. Match what the searcher wants.
  2. Be the local expert. Google rewards real experience. Your own visits, photos, times and tips beat generic text that could be on any site.
  3. Be thorough without padding. Guides often do well at 1,000–1,800 words. Cover the follow-up questions people ask.
  4. Place the keyword naturally: title, web address, description, first paragraph and one heading. Never repeat it just to repeat it.
  5. Make it easy to scan: headings, short paragraphs and lists. Most visitors are on phones.
  6. Use original, compressed, described images.
  7. Link well. Link to related Tandem posts, and to a trusted official source when you quote a fact.
  8. Keep it fresh and accurate. Update timings, seasons and rules. Wrong information costs trust, and trust is what ranks.
  9. Use local signals. Mention real landmarks and distances from Bengaluru. Keep the name, address and phone number the same everywhere, including the Google Business Profile.
  10. Be consistent. One or two good posts a week beats ten in one day. Build a cluster of posts around "Nandi Hills" that link to each other.
  11. Earn links. Share with travel bloggers, Instagram creators and local pages. Good posts get mentioned.

Avoid: copying text or pictures from other sites, stuffing keywords, publishing very short posts, buying links, guessing at facts, and changing a web address after publishing.

8. Before-you-publish checklist

  • The title is clear and includes the main keyword where it reads naturally.
  • The opening paragraph mentions the keyword and says what the reader will get.
  • There are headings, short paragraphs and, where useful, lists.
  • All timings, prices, distances and names are confirmed.
  • There is a cover image, and it has a description.
  • Every picture inside the story has a description, and each is under about 500 KB.
  • There is at least one link to another Tandem page, and links use natural words.
  • Google title (50–60), Google description (110–155) and web address are filled in.
  • The checklist panel is mostly green, and you know why any item is not.
  • You read it aloud and checked spelling.
  • You opened the preview on your phone.
  • No text or pictures were copied from elsewhere.

9. If something goes wrong

ProblemWhat to do
I cannot sign in even with the right passwordYour account may not have the Blogger role. Ask the site owner to set your role to Blogger.
An image will not uploadIt is probably over 8 MB or the wrong type (for example an iPhone HEIC photo). Shrink it or save it as a JPG.
My post does not show on the siteCheck Status is Published and the Publish date and time is not in the future. The State column should say Live.
The preview link shows "not found"You must be signed in. Open it from the Open the post link while signed in.
I lost some textPress Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on a Mac). Then use Save and continue editing regularly.
The checklist panel is missingRefresh the page. If it is still missing, tell the site owner which browser you use.

For anything else, message the site owner and say which post you were editing and what you saw on the screen.
